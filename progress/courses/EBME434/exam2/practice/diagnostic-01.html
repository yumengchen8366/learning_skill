    1. Constant-rate infusion, one compartment

    10 points · ~7 min · units · single-compartment model · nondimensionalization

    An antibiotic is given by continuous IV infusion at a constant rate R = 120 mg/hr into a single well-mixed compartment of volume V = 40 L. Elimination is first order with rate constant k = 0.25 hr−1. There is no drug in the body at t = 0.

    1. Write the mass-balance ODE for the concentration C(t) (mg/L), with its initial condition. State the units of every term in the equation and show they agree.
    2. Solve the ODE for C(t).
    3. What is the steady-state concentration Css? How long does it take to reach 95% of Css? Express that time both in hours and as a multiple of the time constant.
    4. Nondimensionalize the equation using τ = kt and θ = C/Css. Write the resulting equation and explain in one sentence why this is the "natural" choice of scales.

    2. Two-compartment kinetics: time scales

    10 points · ~8 min · eigenvalues · fast/slow phases

    A drug distributes between a central compartment (1) and a peripheral compartment (2). Using amounts A1, A2 (mg), the model is

    dA1/dt = −(k12 + ke)A1 + k21A2 dA2/dt = k12A1 − k21A2

    with k12 = 0.3 hr−1, k21 = 0.1 hr−1, ke = 0.2 hr−1, and a bolus A1(0) = 500 mg, A2(0) = 0.

    1. Write the system as dA/dt = M A and give the matrix M with numbers.
    2. Find the two eigenvalues (show the characteristic equation; 3 significant figures is fine).
    3. Give the fast and slow time constants in hours. Which eigenvalue governs the terminal (late-time) decay of a semi-log plot of A1(t)?
    4. Sketch (describe in words if typing) what ln A1 vs t looks like, and explain in one sentence why the two-compartment model is "stiffer" than a one-compartment model with k = 0.2 hr−1.

    4. Two competing cell populations

    12 points · ~10 min · fixed points · Jacobian · classification · feedback

    Two cell populations x and y (dimensionless densities) compete for the same nutrient:

    dx/dt = x(3 − x − 2y) dy/dt = y(2 − x − y)
    1. Find all fixed points.
    2. Write the Jacobian in general form.
    3. Classify the stability of each fixed point (node/saddle/spiral, stable/unstable) using tr and det, or the eigenvalues.
    4. In one or two sentences: what does the long-term outcome depend on, and what is the biological name for this behaviour?
    5. Is the effect of y on x a positive or negative feedback? Is the effect of x on itself (at small x) positive or negative? One phrase each.

    6. Setting up a diffusion–reaction model

    10 points · ~7 min · control volume · Fick's law · IC/BC · units

    A flat hydrogel scaffold of thickness L is seeded uniformly with cells. Its left face (x = 0) sits in culture medium that holds the glucose concentration fixed at C0. Its right face (x = L) is bonded to an impermeable plate. The cells consume glucose at a rate proportional to the local glucose concentration (rate constant k). At t = 0 the scaffold contains no glucose.

    1. Starting from a mass balance on a control volume of thickness Δx and area A, and Fick's law J = −D ∂C/∂x, derive the governing PDE for C(x, t). (Short derivation — show the limit Δx → 0.)
    2. Write the initial condition and both boundary conditions. Name the type of each boundary condition.
    3. If [C] = mol/m3, [x] = m, [t] = s: give the units of D, J, and k, and check that all terms in your PDE have the same units.
    4. Suppose instead the right face were in contact with a second, well-stirred bath in which glucose is carried away with a mass-transfer coefficient kc (m/s) and far-field concentration 0. Write the new boundary condition at x = L and name its type.
